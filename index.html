The International Workshop on Security for AI Systems and AI for Systems Security (AISEC 2026)

AISEC 2026 is organized with the 2026 International Conference on Next Generation AI Systems (NGEN-AI 2026), taking place on 1–4 September 2026 in Trento, Italy, and online over Zoom.

The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of artificial intelligence and cybersecurity, covering both security for AI systems and AI-based methods for strengthening the security of modern software, network, cloud, edge, IoT, and cyber-physical systems.

Call for Papers

Submit original work on securing AI systems and using AI to secure complex digital systems.

AISEC 2026 invites researchers, practitioners, and industry experts to submit original contributions addressing the increasingly important relationship between artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. As AI systems become embedded in critical software, cloud, edge, IoT, autonomous, and cyber-physical infrastructures, new security risks emerge around robustness, privacy, trust, misuse, accountability, and operational resilience. At the same time, AI is creating new opportunities for threat detection, vulnerability analysis, incident response, secure software engineering, and adaptive defense.

The workshop welcomes theoretical, empirical, experimental, and applied work, including full research papers, short papers, position papers, case studies, tool demonstrations, datasets, benchmarks, and lessons learned from real-world deployments.

Scope

AISEC 2026 focuses on two closely connected research directions. The first is security for AI systems, covering methods, architectures, tools, and evaluation approaches for protecting AI models, AI-enabled applications, LLM-based systems, autonomous agents, and data-driven infrastructures against attacks, failures, leakage, manipulation, and misuse. This includes questions related to adversarial robustness, model integrity, data poisoning, prompt injection, privacy risks, secure MLOps, trustworthy deployment, explainability, governance, and compliance.

The second direction is AI for systems security, focusing on how machine learning, generative AI, agentic AI, federated learning, and other intelligent techniques can improve the security of software-intensive systems. Relevant work may address intrusion detection, malware analysis, vulnerability discovery, secure software engineering, digital forensics, risk assessment, security monitoring, incident response, and protection of cloud, edge, IoT, networked, and cyber-physical systems.

Accepted papers will be published in the NGEN-AI 2026 main conference proceedings in Springer CCIS series, indexed in leading databases including SCOPUS, Norwegian Register for Scientific Journals and Series, DBLP, EI Compendex, INSPEC, SCImago, zbMATH, and the Japanese Science and Technology Agency (JST).

Topics of Interest

Adversarial attacks, defenses, and robustness of AI systems
Data poisoning, model stealing, model inversion, and membership inference
Security and safety of LLMs, generative AI, and agentic AI systems
Prompt injection, jailbreaks, tool misuse, and secure AI agents
Privacy-preserving, trustworthy, explainable, and accountable AI security
Secure MLOps, AI lifecycle security, and model supply-chain protection
Secure deployment of AI in cloud, edge, IoT, and cyber-physical systems
AI-based intrusion detection, anomaly detection, and threat intelligence
AI for malware analysis, vulnerability discovery, and digital forensics
AI-assisted secure software engineering and code security analysis
Federated, distributed, and privacy-preserving intelligence for cybersecurity
Security of autonomous systems, robotics, smart cities, and critical infrastructure
Benchmarks, datasets, testbeds, and reproducibility for AI security research
Human, organizational, legal, ethical, and governance aspects of secure AI systems
